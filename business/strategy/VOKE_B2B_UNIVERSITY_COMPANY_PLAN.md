# Voke B2B Plan: Universities and Companies

**Purpose:** Sell Voke as an interview-readiness product to universities and companies.  
**Focus:** No third-party promotion platforms.  
**Time frame:** First 90 days, then scale over one placement cycle.

---

## 1. The simple strategy

Voke should sell to organisations that already have a real reason to improve interview readiness:

- **Universities** want more students to clear internships and placements.
- **Companies** want early-career talent, interns, trainees, or agency candidates to communicate better in interviews and become job-ready faster.

Voke is not selling “AI mock interviews.” It is selling a measurable outcome:

> **More interview-ready students and candidates, with less manual coaching work.**

The first goal is not to sell to hundreds of institutions. The first goal is to run **three small, measurable pilots**, prove usage and feedback improvement, and turn them into paid cohort contracts.

---

## 2. Who will buy Voke?

### A. Universities and colleges - primary market

**Buyer:** Training & Placement Officer (TPO), Career Services Head, Dean, Employability Cell, or faculty coordinator.  
**Champion:** Student placement committee, coding club lead, department representative.  
**Users:** Final-year students, pre-final-year internship applicants, and students with interviews in the next 30-90 days.

### Their real problems

1. Many students know the syllabus but cannot explain projects, answer HR questions, or communicate their technical thinking.
2. Placement teams cannot give every student several personal mock interviews.
3. Colleges need stronger placement outcomes and employability evidence for students, parents, and accreditation/placement reporting.
4. Faculty and placement teams need to know where students are weak - technical explanation, confidence, speech, body language, or resume quality.

### Why a university will take Voke

| University need | Voke answer |
|---|---|
| Scale mock interview practice to a large batch | Students practise on demand instead of waiting for a faculty panel. |
| Improve placement readiness | Text, video, audio/body-language and company-style interview practice. |
| Identify training gaps | Aggregate cohort report: participation, practice completion and common gaps. |
| Need an affordable placement tool | Bulk seats cost less than buying individual student plans. |
| Need low administrative effort | Voke provides access codes, onboarding, usage report and support. |

### Best university customers to approach first

- Engineering colleges with 300-2,000 placement-eligible students.
- Colleges where internship or placement drives begin within the next 90 days.
- Institutions with active placement committees.
- Tier 2/3 colleges where students have fewer opportunities for high-quality individual mock interviews.
- Private universities and autonomous colleges first; they usually have faster approval processes than large public institutions.

---

### B. Companies - secondary market

Do not begin by selling Voke to a company simply because it is interviewing applicants. A company may prefer to assess candidates as they are. Instead, sell Voke to companies that are responsible for making people job-ready.

### Best company customer types

| Company type | Buyer | What they need Voke for |
|---|---|---|
| Companies with campus-hire / graduate programmes | Campus hiring or L&D lead | Prepare selected interns, graduate trainees, and pre-joining hires for professional communication. |
| Staffing and recruitment agencies | Founder, delivery manager, recruitment head | Help candidates prepare before client interviews; reduce repeated rejections and recruiter coaching time. |
| IT services / BPO / consulting firms | L&D or talent-development lead | Improve interview and client-communication readiness for freshers or internal role changes. |
| Companies running CSR employability programmes | CSR or programme lead | Give students free structured interview practice as a measurable employability intervention. |

### Why a company will take Voke

| Company problem | Voke value |
|---|---|
| Recruiters repeatedly coach candidates before client interviews | Candidates practise independently before recruiter review. |
| Senior employees spend too much time doing basic mock interviews | Voke handles the first practice and feedback loop 24/7. |
| Graduate hires need confidence before customer-facing work | Video and communication practice gives a safe rehearsal environment. |
| L&D wants measurable training activity | Cohort-level activity and readiness data can be reported. |
| Employer wants a stronger campus / CSR story | Offer students a real career-preparation benefit without promising a job. |

### Critical company policy

Voke must never claim it can determine hiring decisions or guarantee a job. For recruitment use, the company should not receive an individual candidate’s private interview recording, score, or answer without that candidate’s explicit consent. Default reporting is aggregate only.

## 3. What exactly should Voke offer?

### 1. Free Diagnostic Mock - the only free offer needed

Give each pilot user:

- **1 Text Interview**
- **1 Pro Video Interview (10 minutes)**
- Basic improvement summary and a clear recommendation for the next practice.

### Why this is the correct free amount

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| It proves Voke's core value | The student experiences both answer-quality feedback and video/body-language feedback. |
| It creates a useful baseline | The first mock becomes the student's starting score and improvement plan. |
| It protects unit economics | Internal AI cost is only INR 2.89 per student: INR 0.17 Text + INR 2.72 Pro Video. |
| More free mocks reduce urgency | Giving 2-4 video mocks for free lets many students get enough value without upgrading or using their institution's paid seats. |
| It is easy to communicate | “One complete interview diagnostic, free.” |

**Do not give the current full four-credit free bundle in B2B pilots.** Its INR 5.78 cost is still low, but it is double the cost without being necessary to prove the product. Use the extra free credits only as a targeted reward after webinar attendance, feedback, or a second practice milestone.

### 2. Paid Voke Pro Cohort Pack

For students who need regular practice:

- 4 Pro Video Interview credits
- 3 Text Interview credits
- Full video/body-language feedback
- Detailed audio/video performance reports
- Role-based practice templates

### 3. Paid Voke Elite Cohort Pack

For students with interviews close by or more demanding roles:

- 4 Elite Company Credits, up to 16 interview rounds
- 2 Pro Video Interview credits
- Body-language feedback in Round 1 and HR rounds
- Code IDE practice
- Resume optimisation
- Company and role-specific interview preparation

### 4. Organisation layer - what makes it B2B

Every university or company deal should include:

- Named cohort / department / programme landing page
- Access code or bulk seat allocation
- One 30-45 minute launch workshop
- Simple learner onboarding guide
- Weekly aggregate usage report during the programme
- Closing cohort report: registrations, activations, completed mocks, common skill gaps, and feedback themes
- Dedicated email/WhatsApp support contact for the coordinator

Do not promise a custom dashboard, SSO, API integration, or deep customisation in the first pilot unless it already exists and can be delivered safely.

## 6. How the university sales process works

### Step 1: Find the right contact

Contact one decision-maker and one student champion:

- TPO / Career Services Head
- Dean or department head
- Student placement committee leader

### Step 2: Ask for a 20-minute discovery call

Do not start by discussing price. Ask:

1. How many students are placement-eligible in the next 90 days?
2. What are the most common reasons students fail interviews?
3. How are mock interviews conducted today, and how many students receive them?
4. When are the next internship, placement, or company drives?
5. Does the college have a training budget, or must students pay individually?

### Step 3: Offer the controlled 14-day pilot

Explain that the university will get a cohort report, while individual answers remain private unless the student consents to sharing them.

### Step 4: Review results and close

At the end of the pilot, present:

- Number invited
- Number registered
- First-mock completion rate
- Number who practised more than once
- Common improvement areas
- Student feedback
- Recommended paid cohort size and plan mix

### University outreach email

**Subject:** Free 14-day interview-readiness pilot for [College] students

> Hi [Name], Voke helps placement-eligible students practise technical, HR and video interviews before real company drives. We would like to run a no-cost, 14-day pilot for up to 100 [College] students: one complete interview diagnostic per student, a launch workshop, and an aggregate readiness report for your placement team. There is no long-term commitment. May we schedule a 20-minute call to see if this fits your upcoming placement cycle?

## 7. How the company sales process works

### Step 1: Pick the correct use case

Use one of these opening messages:

- **Recruitment agency:** “Help candidates practise before client interviews.”
- **Graduate programme:** “Prepare interns and trainees to explain projects and communicate professionally.”
- **L&D:** “Give freshers repeatable interview and client-communication practice.”
- **CSR employability programme:** “Offer measurable career-preparation access to student beneficiaries.”

### Step 2: Run a small paid or sponsored cohort

For companies, avoid an open-ended free pilot. Offer either:

- 20 sponsored Free Diagnostic Mock seats for a discovery cohort, or
- A paid 50-seat Workforce-Readiness Pack.

The company has a commercial budget and needs to see serious intent. A limited discovery cohort is enough to demonstrate the product.

### Company outreach email

**Subject:** Reduce manual candidate interview coaching with a structured readiness cohort

> Hi [Name], Voke is an AI interview-practice platform for early-career talent. It helps candidates practise technical explanations, HR answers, voice/video communication and project walkthroughs before recruiter or client interviews. We can run a 50-seat readiness cohort with a launch session and weekly aggregate usage report. The goal is to reduce repeated basic coaching and make candidates better prepared before human interview time is used. Could we understand your current candidate-preparation process in a short call?

### Step 3: Sell the business value, not technology

On every company call, quantify their current manual effort:

- Number of candidates coached per month
- Average minutes a recruiter spends per candidate
- Number of candidates rejected due to communication/readiness gaps
- Number of client interview rejections or no-shows

Example: if a recruiter spends 30 minutes preparing 100 candidates, that is 50 recruiter-hours. Voke should be positioned as the first practice layer; recruiters focus their time on final candidates and role-specific coaching.

## 8. 90-day execution plan

### Days 1-14: Prepare the B2B product

- Create one university landing page and one company landing page.
- Build unique cohort codes and basic tracking: organisation, student sign-up, first mock, paid upgrade, completed mocks.
- Prepare a one-page PDF, 10-slide sales deck, onboarding message and sample cohort report.
- Write privacy terms, consent language, invoice template and pilot agreement.
- Prepare role templates: Software Engineer, Data Analyst, Product, HR, and Consulting.

### Days 15-30: Sell pilots

- Build a list of 30 universities and 20 target companies/recruitment agencies.
- Send personalised outreach to 10 universities and 10 companies every week.
- Book at least 10 discovery calls.
- Close 2 university pilots and 1 company discovery cohort.

### Days 31-60: Deliver and measure

- Launch the pilots with a live onboarding workshop.
- Send weekly progress updates to the coordinator.
- Interview 10 active students/candidates to understand value and objections.
- Collect anonymised testimonials with permission.
- Identify which users need Pro and which need Elite.

### Days 61-90: Convert and expand

- Present closing report and paid recommendation to each pilot customer.
- Close one university bulk contract of at least 100 seats.
- Close one company cohort contract of at least 50 seats.
- Turn results into two case studies.
- Use those case studies to approach the next 50 institutions/companies.

| Metric | University pilot target | Company cohort target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Invitation to registration | 50%+ | 60%+ |
| Registration to first mock completion | 40%+ | 60%+ |
| Users completing a second practice | 25%+ | 35%+ |
| Student/candidate satisfaction | 4/5+ | 4/5+ |
| Pilot to paid conversion | 1 paid cohort from every 3 pilots | 1 paid cohort from every 3 discovery cohorts |
| Paid cohort renewal / expansion | 50%+ | 50%+ |

Track these metrics per organisation. Do not judge success by sign-ups alone; a large group that never completes the first mock is not a valuable B2B customer.

## 11. Risks and rules

1. **Do not promise placements or jobs.** Promise interview practice and readiness only.
2. **Do not give unlimited free mocks.** One diagnostic mock is enough to prove value.
3. **Do not offer free pilots larger than 100 university users or 20 company users.** Larger access must be paid.
4. **Do not sell custom enterprise features before validating demand.** Start with cohort codes, reports, workshop and support.
5. **Protect user privacy.** Share aggregate data by default and secure explicit permission for any individual sharing.
6. **Require a committed coordinator.** A pilot without official communication and follow-up will have weak activation.
7. **Ask for payment before bulk activation.** Avoid long credit terms in the first year.
